# Phase 4 baselines (sector level)

Generated by `make backtest` (`src/bsp/evaluation/run_baselines.py`). Every number is out-of-sample: expanding-window walk-forward, and a budget is predicted only from rows whose 12m label was already known on its day 0. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Setup

- Test budgets: the 12 full budgets from FY2012-13 to FY2023-24; interim budgets have no sector budget data and are not predicted. Training grows every year (rows from year t or later are never used; minimum 3 training budgets; rows with `target_complete = False` are excluded from training).
- **Locked hold-out: FY2024-25, FY2025-26 (with their interim budgets); sealed live year: FY2026-27.** The evaluation panel does not load them, and asking for them raises `HoldoutLockedError` until Phase 9 sets `holdout_unlocked` in `config/evaluation.json`.
- Target: 12m excess return of the sector basket over the Nifty 500 (day after the budget to the next budget's day 0).
- **IT Services is the control sector.** Models train on and predict all 15 sectors, but every headline number below uses the 14 budget-sensitive sectors; the control is reported on its own in the last section.
- Portfolios: equal weight, top 3 of the 14 sectors (equal weight = all 14), bought at the close of the day after the budget and sold at the target window's end; 0.2% round-trip cost per window (0.1% each side); cash earns 0% between windows; Sharpe uses risk-free = 0 over the whole span including cash days. The Nifty 500 benchmark is bought and sold over the same windows with the same costs.
- Seed 42; two runs give byte-identical files.
- Limitations: 12 test years, so every statistic is noisy; sector baskets are equal-weight baskets of today's stock list (survivorship, small/mid-cap tilt), so every basket tends to beat the cap-weighted Nifty 500 and the fair bar for a model is the **equal-weight** row; baskets are thin in early years (defence has 2 stocks until 2017, railways 1 until 2018; `reports/sector_basket_report.csv`). Stock-level evaluation is not built, because the stock universe uses listing-date plus liquidity filters only and survivorship bias is not removed (see `reports/data_gaps.md`).

## IT Services (control sector), reported separately

The control sector is not meant to respond to the budget. Per README rule 11, a strong 'budget effect' here is treated as a **leakage alarm**, not a discovery. The alarm rule used: a model's score for the control sector correlates positively with its realised excess return across the test years with one-sided p < 0.05.

| Model | Mean predicted rank (of 15) | Mean realised rank (of 15) | Years in predicted top 3 | Spearman rho | p (one-sided) | Alarm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| last_years_winners | 8.4 | 7.2 | 3 of 12 | 0.224 | 0.242 | no |
| biggest_allocation_increase | 4.3 | 7.2 | 6 of 12 | 0.182 | 0.286 | no |
| ridge | 3.8 | 7.2 | 6 of 12 | -0.273 | 0.804 | no |

No model finds a significant budget effect in the control sector, so there is no leakage alarm from this check.
